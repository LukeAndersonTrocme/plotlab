# Fill

**What it does:** fills each band from **Bands** with a pattern of lines or dots. Darker bands
get denser patterns, so the picture comes back as hatching, like an engraving or a
topographic map with shaded zones. All bands share one grid across the page, so lines in
neighbouring bands line up.

**Analog history:** *To be written with Nick* (engraving and cross-hatching, halftone
screens, Ben Day dots, the old R prototype's banded hatches…).

**What the settings do physically:**
- **Fill with:** lines (hatching) or dots (good for screen printing and letterpress).
- **Pattern:** *Smooth with darkness* changes spacing (and optionally angle) gradually.
  *Cycle directions* switches each band between horizontal, vertical and the diagonals.
- **Darkest / lightest spacing:** how tight the lines are in the darkest and lightest bands.
  Tighter = darker, and a longer plot.
- **Angle / Turn with darkness:** the direction of the lines, and how much it turns from
  light to dark bands.
- **Dot size:** the largest dot; lighter bands get smaller dots.
- **Per-band settings:** give one band its own spacing or angle (band 1 is the lightest).
- **Cross-hatch (cumulative):** darker bands also get the lighter bands' lines, so the
  hatching builds up in layers.
- **Leave the lightest band as paper / Draw band outlines.**
