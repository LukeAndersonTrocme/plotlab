# What plotlab is

## What it does

You give plotlab a picture. It runs the picture through a chain of steps, each doing one
thing: cut it into black and white, sort it into bands from light to dark, fill each band
with hatching, put the lines in a good order for the pen. At the end you get one of these:

- a drawing for a **pen plotter**, with an estimate of how long it will take;
- a **digital image** (PNG or TIFF) or a **digital vector** file (SVG or PDF);
- **print prep**: one file per ink for riso, screen print, relief or film.

The idea is a modular synthesizer. There is a lot going on inside, but you can start from a
ready-made look and turn the knobs to see what happens, without knowing the theory.

## What you need

- **A laptop or desktop browser.** Chrome is the most tested. Safari and Firefox should
  work.
- **An image.** PNG, JPEG, WebP or TIFF. A portrait, or anything with clear light and dark
  areas, works well. If you don't have one handy, plotlab opens on a sample (the Mona Lisa).
- **A phone**, only if you want to look at your projects there (§5.9). The phone can view
  but not edit.

## Where your work is kept

Everything stays in your own browser. Nothing is uploaded. Your projects are stored in the
browser you used to make them, on that computer only.

This has two consequences:

- Clearing your browser's site data erases your projects.
- To keep a project safe, or to move it to another computer or your phone, export it as a
  bundle (§5.9).
