# Your first drawing

This takes about ten minutes. You go from an image to a drawing ready for a plotter, and
see what each step does along the way.

## Open your image

1. Open [lukeandersontrocme.github.io/plotlab](https://lukeandersontrocme.github.io/plotlab/).
   The sample appears with a three-step recipe: **Threshold**, **Centerline**,
   **Stroke order**.
2. Click **Open image, SVG or recipe…** at the top of the sidebar and pick your image. You
   can also drag the file onto the window.

## Pick a look

3. Click **Presets**. Each preset is shown on your own picture. Pick
   **Topographic hatching**. A message says your previous settings were saved as a
   checkpoint.
4. In the recipe strip, click each box in turn: **Source**, then **Levels**, **Bands**,
   **Fill**, **Stroke order**. The canvas shows what that step makes, and the sidebar shows
   its settings.

## Turn a knob

5. Click **Fill** and drag **Darkest spacing**. With **Live** ticked, the drawing updates as
   you drag. Watch the time estimate above the canvas change too: tighter lines take longer
   to plot.
6. Click **?** on the Fill card to read its help. Then click **↺** on the card to put its
   settings back to the defaults.

## Look closer

7. Click **Compare stages**. You see the source and the result side by side, with a divider
   to drag. Try **Lens** and **Boxes**, and **Every step** to see the whole chain at once.
   Click **Compare stages** again to leave.
8. Tick **Show source underneath** to see how the lines sit on your picture.

## Save it

9. Press **⌘S** (Ctrl+S on Windows) to save this version as a checkpoint.

You now have a drawing. To get it onto paper, see §5.7 (paper and pens) and §5.8 (time and
downloads).
